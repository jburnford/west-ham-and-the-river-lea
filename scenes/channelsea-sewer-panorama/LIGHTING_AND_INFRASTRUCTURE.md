## Lighting and ground

`docs/lighting.js` now owns the interpreted overcast setup. Diffuse sky illumination and environment response are stronger, with a lower direct-to-fill ratio and slightly higher exposure. A continuous equirectangular sky shares its northwest bright region with the directional light. Exponential haze matches the horizon radiance. These are visual estimates, not a solved historic time, weather condition or calibrated lighting model.

The river still reflects the actual scene and camera. Fine wet sediment has broader highlights. The terrain shader now confines wet sediment shading to the atlas's riverbed mask: it previously darkened low-lying dry land too, making Mill Meads read as black paving. The author then clarified that undeveloped land should carry grass and weeds. A separate land-cover mask now distinguishes open ground, industrial sites and cultivated allotments. Open ground has a muted mixed grass surface and low coarse vegetation; working sites remain worn grey-brown, and allotments retain cultivated soil. Vegetation excludes mapped road corridors, exposed sediment and working sites. Higher flood-bank ground transitions to grass. The cover and plant density are interpretations, with no species or exact seasonal claim. The new `river-terrain.landcover` asset is rebuilt by the terrain generator. This changes surface interpretation, not the terrain heights or mapped geography.

## Road surfaces: author evidence versus model assignment

The author supplied the period distinction between heavy cartage routes paved with granite setts, possible wood-block shopping streets, water-bound macadam residential streets, and unmade gravel/ash/clinker/earth streets. They specifically advised checking Borough Engineer reports before identifying named wood-block streets, and highlighted adoption status and the unstable marsh subsoil.

The scene uses original code-generated grey/brown macadam, stone setts and clinker/earth textures, with no blacktop, painted markings or electrified tram equipment. Residential routes provisionally use macadam; lanes/paths use earthy aggregate; selected works approaches use setts. These assignments do **not** establish a named street's adoption status or actual paving in a specific year. Wood-block streets are not assigned without further evidence. Named research leads supplied by the author: West Ham Borough Engineer annual reports at Newham Archives, Local Government Board road returns, Howarth and Wilson's 1907 survey and Booth notebooks. These leads were recorded, not newly consulted in this pass.
