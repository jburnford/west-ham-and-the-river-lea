# Coal traffic: steam colliers, Thames lighters and Lea barges

The October 2026 study adds twelve representative craft to the working c1900
scene: two iron screw colliers, five broad Thames lighters, four narrower Lea
barges and a small steam tug. Three transfer groups show coal whipping with a
raised gang stage and basket, a derrick-supported basket, and an interpreted
early grab. A tug and two lighters illustrate onward lighterage; loaded and empty
inland barges continue through Bow Creek, Bow Locks and the Limehouse Cut.
Twenty-three static workers show hauling, shovelling and handling sweeps.

These are vessel types, not identified ships. Their dimensions, colours, loads,
fittings and moorings are authored interpretations. Positions were fitted to the
existing water geometry, not derived from a berth survey. The tableaux deliberately
illustrate the handling vocabulary in the supplied sources; they do not establish
which equipment was used at these exact locations in 1900. Steam and manual gear
appear together as an interpretive study. The static smoke suggests working steam
vessels; cargo transfer and navigation are not animated or simulated.

## Geometry and water

The collier study is 62 × 9.4 m, the Thames lighter 22 × 6 m, the Lea barge
22 × 4.2 m, and the tug 18 × 4.8 m. These are modelling inputs, not historical
measurements, lock-clearance claims or cargo-capacity estimates. Hulls have
submerged bilges, narrowing shoulders, sheer and raised end decks. Cargo wells
are actual holes in the deck, with coamings and floors. Coal combines an uneven
continuous surface with angular chunks; inland loads have three distinct heaps.
The two Thames hatch sections are a form variant, not a universal classification.

All twelve conservative hull envelopes fit their assigned drawn water and remain
separate from each other. The two steam colliers stay in the Thames context reach.
Tidal vessels, gear, workers and smoke follow the existing illustrative tide.
The two Limehouse Cut barges remain at the retained level. Twenty new hatch masks
join the four original barge masks, keeping the water surface out of open cargo
wells. No new river footprints, lock levels or bathymetry are inferred.

## Review and portability

The **Coal traffic** group in the **Fly to** menu provides six views: collier
transfer, coal whipping, the collier pair, tug and tow, laden Lea barge, and empty
Lea barge. With the local server on port 4173, start at
**http://localhost:4173/?view=coal-transfer**. Review screenshots and browser
results are in `review/coal-traffic/`.

The original procedural builder is [coal-vessels.js](../../docs/coal-vessels.js).
Its generated input is [docs/data/coal-traffic.json](../../docs/data/coal-traffic.json).
Rebuild and check after changing the register. The builder verifies archived image
checksums when the local reference archive is present; the committed register also
supports a fresh checkout without those downloads:

```bash
python3 scripts/build_coal_traffic.py
python3 scripts/build_scene_manifest.py
node scripts/check_coal_traffic.mjs
python3 scripts/review_coal_traffic.py --quality lite
python3 scripts/review_coal_traffic.py --quality full --fleet-only
```

The geometry check verifies finite coordinates, actual hull vertices in water,
hatch/mask transforms, correct floating materials and the lower-detail budget.
The fleet adds 158,414 triangles in full mode and 78,306 in lite mode. The browser
review checks destinations, high tide offsets, the Abbey Mills view, console
errors and a narrow viewport. It is a local software-rendering check, not a frame
rate benchmark across devices.

Full-detail vessel views were captured successfully. Whole-district Abbey Mills
screenshot capture at full quality exceeded the 120-second software-rendering
timeout twice, including a run without concurrent export. The full scene reached
the Abbey Mills camera; automated regression captures use lite quality. This
capture limit remains a review-environment limitation and is not a completed
full-quality Abbey Mills performance check.

### Figure and coal refinement, 10 October

Author feedback identified toy-like figures and oversized individual coal pieces.
Workers now have smaller oval heads and flat caps, rounded boots, tapered limbs,
shaped shirt torsos and rolled sleeves. Bent knees, staggered feet and slightly
varied pulling poses replace the rigid, identical stances. Rope ends and tools
meet the figures’ hand positions; the barge boatmen face their sweeps. These remain static
interpretive figures, not portrait reconstructions.

Coal piece radii are authored inputs of 0.018–0.072 m before irregular stretching,
replacing the earlier 0.055–0.255 m inputs with much smaller particles. The mound
surface has shared vertices and smooth normals, with an original seamless
aggregate colour/bump texture to fill the spaces between visible chunks.
The overall pile heights and cargo arrangements are unchanged.
This is a visual correction, not a measured cargo grade. Close reviews use:

```bash
python3 scripts/review_coal_traffic.py --quality full --details
```

The separate `coal-traffic` export layer produces a Blender-importable GLB:

```bash
python3 scripts/export_gltf.py coal-traffic --quality=lite
python3 scripts/check_gltf.py exports/gltf/coal-traffic.glb
```

Exported meshes preserve metre scale, world placement, materials and the existing
British National Grid origin tag. The current exporter batches by material, so
the GLB is a review mesh rather than a vessel-by-vessel editable source scene.
The review export preserves the hull forms and principal handling gear, reduces
coal/rope detail and applies the scene’s usual lite filter for thin box details.
The checked GLB is 8.1 MB, with 31 meshes, 77,730 triangles and four embedded
textures. `--quality=full` requests the denser version. Browser-only tide controls,
water masks, weather shading and point-based smoke are not included.
Blender can refine the hulls and fittings from this geometry;
historical identification and measured plans still require additional evidence.
