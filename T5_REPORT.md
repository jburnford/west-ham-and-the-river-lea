# T5 report: regional water plane extents (issue I5)

3 October 2026. Worktree branch `worktree-agent-adb094c4091a57ad4`.

## Main finding: the brief's premise was partly wrong

The regional water polygons were already inside their channels. Before my change, every regional water polygon lay inside its own reach polygons, and those reach edges are the bank lines the regional bank sections are built from (`check_river_banks.py` asserts the bank footprint never enters the reach polygons). So the measured overshoot was 0.00 m before I changed anything.

The "rectangle sticking out of the Lea at Bromley" (x −660..−630, z 840..890 and beyond) is the head of the **Limehouse Cut** (reach `Water_1895-508`, "six-inch blue water"). That canal really does leave the Lea at Bromley below Bow Locks. It looked like a stray rectangle for two reasons:
- it was drawn in a separate opaque pale-blue material (`0x73979a`) rather than the network's reflective water;
- it begins with a straight cut across the channel where the core network water ends.

Clipping it would have deleted a real canal, so I did not. The pale planes in `lea-west-bank-along-900` (the Cut, parallel to the Lea) and `network-steps-1100-700` (City Mill River) are the same thing: correct extent, wrong material.

There was one real geometric defect. Regional water overlapped the network's **tidal polygons** by 588.6 m² in 9 places at junctions. The tidal polygons are drawn at the same level with `materials.water`, so these were coplanar double planes with straight edges.

## What I changed

1. **`docs/river-system.js`**: the regional water now uses the shared `materials.water` instead of its own `MeshStandardMaterial({color: 0x73979a})`. This was the actual cause of I5's visible defects (the pale opaque colour and the straight seam), so the material had to change.
   - Effects of sharing the material: the regional water now gets the network's reflection and sediment shader, and is batched with the core water (one fewer draw call). It is hidden during the reflection pass like all other water. In the flood view it switches to the plain flood colour along with the rest of the water. It is still static: the tide moves only `materials.tidalWater` meshes, so the regional channels stay out of the tide and the flood domain.
2. **`scripts/build_river_system.py`**: a new `drawn_water` for `waterPolygons`. It is `display_water` minus everything else the scene draws as network water at the same level (tidal polygons, marsh-ditch render polygons, terrain pools, buffered 1 cm so the edges meet flush). Two assertions back it: the drawn water stays inside the mapped reaches, and it has no overlap with network water.
   - `display_water` itself is deliberately unchanged, because it also drives `coreBedCorrections` and the `baseGround`/`regionalGround` cuts. Under the trimmed areas the bed stays lowered and covered by the network's own water.
3. **`docs/data/river-system-1900.json`**: regenerated by the full builder. Only `waterPolygons` and two `inputHashes` changed. The two hashes are `ground-plan.json` and `factory-buildings.json`, which were already stale on `main` (see "Checks"). All six binaries (`.f32`, `.u32`, `.silt`, `.cover`, `.faces.f32`, `.face-uv.f32`) are byte-identical, and so are `reaches`, `coreBedCorrections`, `baseGround`, `regionalGround` and `bankSections`. An unmodified rebuild changes only those two hashes, so the builder is deterministic.
4. `scripts/check_river_system.py`: not changed. No count assertion depends on `waterPolygons`.

I did not drop slivers. The 11 small pieces (0.1–18 m²) sit next to the network channel, over bed lowered to about −0.7 by `coreBedCorrections`. Removing them would have exposed dry holes in the bed. Two pieces disappeared only because they lay entirely inside tidal polygons.

## Decisions for the parent

1. **Hash cascade.** Any byte change to `river-system-1900.json` breaks the provenance hash recorded by `elevation-audit.json`, `elevation-trial.json`, `main-landscape-1900.json` and `lower-lea-region/landscape-1900.json`. Of those checks, only `check_regional_elevation.py` was passing on `main`. Their content does not depend on `waterPolygons`, so a hash-only refresh, or a rebuild after T2–T4 merge, would be correct. Merge order with T2/T3/T4 (which rebuild `main-landscape-1900.*` against the old hash) matters.
2. **Rebuilds are not isolated.** My rebuild also refreshed the stale `ground-plan.json` and `factory-buildings.json` hashes. T1 and T6 change those files in other worktrees, so after they merge `river-system-1900.json` will need another rebuild (its binaries do depend on factory footprints via `flat_ground`).
