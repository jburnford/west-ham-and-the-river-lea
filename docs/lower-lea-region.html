Coverage and evidence

Explore the landscape in 3D

Street levels anchor developed ground. Marsh lanes can guide adjacent ground where evidence is sparse, with an uncertain local height difference. No fixed fill correction is assumed. Where identified marsh has no better evidence, about 12 ft in the historical datum is the working prior. Earlier marsh levels can provide lower-bound estimates where the land surface survives; later development may raise it.

Connections to review

Nearby polygons are candidates for checking, not confirmed culverts or open channels. A mapped contact may contain a lock, weir or mill control.

Mills, gates and navigation

Mill passages, sluices and weirs can hold water upstream and limit discharge. Each needs its own opening, bypass and overtopping assessment. A mill name alone does not establish an operating watermill.

Navigation interfaces need separate review even where the mapped water polygons touch.

The 1898 Three Mills junction tide-gate proposal was rejected; it is not added as a barrier. Exact control locations and map dates remain under review.
